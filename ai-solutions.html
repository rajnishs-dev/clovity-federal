AI-assisted operations, inside a governed environment

We add AI to Jira, Confluence and JSM only after your security team approves it.

We help agencies add AI to Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management only after it is approved, bounded and logged. Your security team decides. We do the groundwork.

Build faster. Scale smarter. Deliver with governed AI.

Delivery teams already plan, write, build and support their work in Atlassian. Where your deployment offers AI features, we fit them into that flow at the points where a person already reviews the work. The result is help with the busywork, not a new path for your data.

  • Plan and groom work in Jira
  • Draft and find pages in Confluence
  • Review changes in Bitbucket
  • Resolve requests in JSM

Governed AI expertise

AI features vary by deployment. Commercial cloud, Government Cloud and Data Center do not offer the same set, so we start from what exists in yours.

Deployment-aware advice

We don't assume a commercial cloud feature, such as Rovo, is available in Government Cloud. We confirm availability for your deployment before it goes in a plan.

Platform features first

Native Atlassian capabilities come before third-party apps. Any app is vetted on its own, because the Government Cloud authorization does not cover Marketplace apps.

Automation with guardrails

Rules-based Jira and JSM automation often does the job without AI. Where AI adds value, we connect it to the same permissions and audit trail.

Six checks before AI is switched on

Compliance with clear scope: Government Cloud's FedRAMP Moderate authorization covers Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management. It does not extend to commercial cloud or Marketplace apps. So every AI feature or app gets the same six checks. If one fails, the feature stays off.

  1. Use case on the inventory

    Each use case is written down with its purpose, owner and users, ready for your agency AI inventory.

  2. Approval before enablement

    Your security and privacy leads sign off in writing. Nothing turns on by default.

  3. Data stays in bounds

    We trace where prompts and content go, and confirm the feature respects existing permissions and your authorization boundary.

  4. Availability confirmed

    Features differ between commercial cloud, Government Cloud and Data Center. We confirm what exists in yours.

  5. A human signs off

    AI drafts. People review and decide. No output is published or acted on without a person in the loop.

  6. Usage on the record

    We set up the logging the platform offers and document it, so AI use can be reviewed like any other activity.

Six pilots worth running first

Each one is low risk, easy to check and simple to switch off. Each one targets a benefit your program already tracks.

Faster service delivery

JSM agents see a suggested knowledge article next to each request and decide whether to send it. We start with one queue and the knowledge base spaces you already maintain.

Centralized knowledge

Plain-language search across Confluence that only returns what the user can already open. Staff find the policy or runbook without knowing which space it lives in.

Improved visibility

A first-draft status report built from live Jira data. The program owner edits and approves it before it goes to leadership.

Standardized workflows

Stale, duplicate or unassigned backlog items are flagged on a set schedule for a person to review. Teams spend less time grooming and more time delivering.

Better collaboration

A summary of a long ticket or page thread, with the source one click away. New reviewers and on-call staff catch up without reading every comment.

Better governance

A short pilot review with your security team: what data the feature touched, who used it and what to change before it goes wider.
