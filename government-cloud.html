Atlassian Government Cloud, set up for your security review

We check whether AGC fits your data, then configure and document it for your ATO.

Atlassian Government Cloud (AGC) is Atlassian's FedRAMP Moderate authorized cloud for U.S. government, run separately from its commercial cloud. We check whether it fits your data, then configure and document it for your ATO.

FedRAMP Moderate Government Cloud

The Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management your teams know, inside an authorized boundary. Here is what that gives you, and what we set up.

Secure cloud-based collaboration

Jira and Confluence for planning and records, with access set by role and need-to-know.

  • Least-privilege permission schemes
  • Space and page restrictions
  • Change history on every item

Government IT service delivery

Jira Service Management for requests, incidents and changes, with each approval kept on the record.

  • Access request workflows
  • Change approvals with history
  • Self-service portal and SLAs

Enterprise governance and administration

Identity through your agency IdP, tight admin roles and audit logs your security team can review.

  • SAML SSO and SCIM provisioning
  • Separate, limited admin roles
  • Audit log review and export

Compliance with clear scope

Know the boundary before you plan inside it. This is the scope as Atlassian announced it in March 2025. A site on Atlassian's commercial cloud is not FedRAMP Moderate, and we will not call it that.

Inside the authorization

  • FedRAMP Moderate authorization, granted March 2025 and listed on the FedRAMP Marketplace
  • Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management
  • An environment run separately from Atlassian's commercial cloud

Outside it, so check first

  • Atlassian's commercial cloud sites
  • Marketplace apps. Each one has to be checked on its own.
  • FedRAMP High and DoD IL5. As of the March 2025 announcement, these were goals, not granted. We'll tell you plainly where AGC stands for your impact level.
  • AI features. These vary by deployment, so confirm what your site offers before you plan around them.
  • Your ATO. AGC's authorization supports it, but your agency still issues it.

Check the fit before you commit

Our readiness check finds the gaps, friction points, security needs and dependencies that decide whether AGC works for you. We work through these questions with your program and security leads, and you leave with a written fit assessment and a plan that runs from setup through renewal.

  • What impact level is your data categorized at?
  • Which apps and integrations can't you live without?
  • Do users sign in with PIV/CAC through an agency IdP?
  • Which features do your teams rely on today?
  • What do your records and retention rules require?
  • Who will own the ATO update for the move?

Assess, architect, operationalize, evolve

Four steps from first look to a Government Cloud site your security team can stand behind.

  1. Assess

    Your data, apps, integrations and features reviewed against AGC's current scope.

    • Data categorization review
    • App and integration inventory
    • Feature gap list

  2. Architect

    A target site designed around your mission workflows, with security controls planned in.

    • Identity via your agency IdP
    • Least-privilege admin roles
    • Mapping to NIST SP 800-53

  3. Operationalize

    The site configured, data moved in tested waves and the baseline written down.

    • SAML SSO and SCIM set up
    • Audit log export confirmed
    • Configuration records for your ISSO

  4. Evolve

    Ongoing support that keeps the site inside its approved baseline as AGC changes.

    • Periodic access reviews
    • Audit log checks for ConMon
    • Release and scope tracking

Data Center ends in March 2029. Plan the move now.

Atlassian is retiring Data Center. New-customer sales ended in March 2026, renewals for existing customers end in March 2028, and end of life follows in March 2029. Legacy Server is already out of support. We move you to cloud with data governance and security controls planned first, not patched in later.

  • Data governance first. Decide what moves, what is archived and what your retention rules require before any data leaves.
  • Controls rebuilt, not assumed. Permissions, groups and sign-in are re-mapped for the new site and documented.
  • Every app vetted. Each app is checked for AGC availability and your security review, or replaced with native features.
  • Test runs first. Rehearse with real data in a non-production site, then validate with user acceptance testing.
  • Rollback ready. Your source instance stays intact until you confirm the new site, with a rollback plan on paper.
